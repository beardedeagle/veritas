# Veritas Trademark Policy

## Status and scope

This document is the normative trademark policy of the Veritas project. It states what the Veritas name and logo cover, who may use them and on what conditions, what a fork or redistributor must and must not do, how a request to use the mark is granted or refused, and how an objection is raised and answered. It is issued under **ADR-045** (Apache-2.0 with a project trademark policy) and it is the instrument that carries the enforcement; [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) is the decision record and rationale that produced it.

The policy is **normative without registration.** It binds the project and binds every recipient and every derivative of the Work, and it requires no filing to have effect. **The project asserts no trademark registration, claims none, and no artefact of the project may assert one.** The ™ symbol is not used anywhere in the project's own artefacts.

## 1. Scope

**1.1 What the mark covers.** The word "Veritas" in plain text and in the distinctive wordmark form; the Veritas logo and the project mark variants; the colour palette, typography, iconography and motion of the LiveView design system as a whole (the look and feel); the names of any first-party distribution, including any prefixed or suffixed variant such as "Veritas Enterprise", "Veritas Cloud", "Veritas Pro" or "Veritas LTS"; and the project's domain names and project URLs.

**1.2 What the mark does not cover.** The Apache-2.0 Licence, the source code, the documentation content, the design of the domain model, the protocol specifications, the test suites, and the accumulated know-how are **not** trademarks and are licensed under the Licence without restriction. This policy narrows nothing in the copyright or patent grants.

**1.3 Relationship to the Apache-2.0 Licence.** The Licence's own Section 6 grants no permission to use the Licensor's trade names, trademarks, service marks or product names, except as required for reasonable and customary use in describing the origin of the Work and reproducing the content of the NOTICE file. **The trademark position is separate from, and does not narrow, the copyright grant, the patent grant, or the Apache-2.0 licence terms.** Nothing in this policy conditions the right to use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense or sell the source code. Section 4 below is a trademark obligation only.

**1.4 No representation of registration.** Neither this policy, nor the NOTICE file, nor any other artefact of the project may state or imply that Veritas is a registered trademark, that the project holds a registration, or that it is entitled to the ™ symbol.

## 2. What is granted, and to whom

**2.1 The project and its maintainers.** The project and its maintainers may use the mark in any way, including in the product name, the binaries, the container images, the documentation, the marketing, the package metadata and the project's own infrastructure.

**2.2 Official distribution channels.** The release artefact, the package managers, the container registries and the project's own documentation may carry the mark, on these conditions: distribution remains **free of charge**; no licence-gated, subscription-gated, metered or otherwise paid variant of Veritas is offered under the mark; modifications are disclosed as Section 4(c) of the Licence requires; and no compatibility, endorsement or certification claim is made without written permission.

**2.3 Reasonable and customary use.** Use of the mark in describing the origin of the Work, and reproduction of the NOTICE content, is permitted, because Section 6 contemplates exactly that. A referential phrase such as "for use with Veritas" is permitted where it accurately describes the relationship and does not imply sponsorship.

**2.4 Grants are specific.** A permission to use the mark granted under §6 is a short, revocable, non-exclusive permission naming the permitted use, the materials it applies to and the conditions, with an expiry. It is not a licence of the mark, it does not permit sublicensing, and prior conduct never implies a grant.

**2.5 No grant by implication.** A redistribution, a fork, a publication, a conference talk, a blog post, a package listing or a compatibility claim never acquires a right to use the mark.

## 3. What is reserved

**3.1 Reserved for the project.** The mark as described in §1.1 is reserved. The project uses it as a source identifier and as a brand for the official distribution.

**3.2 Reserved against confusingly similar use.** No party may use the mark, the wordmark, the logo, the look and feel, or any confusingly similar mark in a way likely to cause confusion about origin, sponsorship, affiliation or certification.

**3.3 Reserved names and identifiers.** The project's domain names and project URLs, and the project's issue tracker, mailing list and continuous-integration service, may not be used by a third party as its own front door without the project's agreement.

**3.4 Reserved claims.** No party may claim that its product is Veritas, is endorsed by, sponsored by, affiliated with, certified by or is a drop-in replacement for Veritas, except with the project's written permission, and, for certification claims, a passed conformance suite.

**3.5 The only accepted limit.** A fork may be sold, and may be sold commercially, under another name. That is the accepted state of open source and this policy does not restrict it. **What the policy prevents is the sale of anything called Veritas.** The obligation it imposes is a **rebrand obligation**, not a fork prohibition, and the project welcomes a properly rebranded fork: a user who found the product worth maintaining.

## 4. Redistributor obligations

**4.1 Retain the notice.** A redistribution of the Work, in source or binary form, must retain the Licence text, the NOTICE file and the attribution notices required by Section 4(d) of the Licence.

**4.2 Disclose modifications.** Modifications must be carried as a notice, in the manner Section 4(c) of the Licence prescribes, and must not be represented as the Work itself.

**4.3 Rebrand.** A derivative or fork must rename: the executable and command names; the package and container image names; the configuration prefix; the telemetry service name; the OIDC client identifier and issuer; the GraphQL schema namespace where it carries the product name; the database and audit-chain identifiers; and every user-facing string, including the window title and the marketing.

**4.4 The narrow survivable use.** The word "Veritas" may survive only inside an attribution notice in documentation — for example, "Based on Veritas, © 2026 the Veritas contributors, licensed under the Apache License 2.0" — and never as the product's name, its window title, or its marketing. An attribution notice in a changelog, a NOTICE file or a credits section is permitted; a product name, a class name, a container tag, a configuration prefix or a CLI command is not.

**4.5 Do not present as Veritas.** A redistribution or a derivative must not present itself as Veritas, Veritas Enterprise, Veritas Cloud, or any other Veritas-branded offering, and must not use the mark or any confusingly similar mark.

**4.6 Do not imply endorsement.** No compatibility, endorsement, sponsorship or certification claim may be made without written permission, and a certification claim additionally requires a passed conformance suite.

**4.7 Do not misrepresent the project's own commitments.** A derivative may not describe itself as "the free forever, never sold" Veritas. The project's commitment in §7 is a commitment of the project, and a fork that charges for Veritas-branded software misrepresents it.

**4.8 What a fork need not do.** A fork need not contribute back, need not use the same licence, need not publish its changes, need not keep any Veritas behaviour, and need not seek or hold permission. The obligations above are the whole of what the policy asks.

## 5. How a request to use the mark is granted or refused

**5.1 By written request.** A request names the party, the permitted use, the materials it applies to, the duration, and the conditions.

**5.2 A grant.** The project may grant a specific, revocable, non-exclusive, time-limited permission, in writing. A grant does not license the mark, does not permit sublicensing, and does not create any expectation of a further grant.

**5.3 A refusal.** The project may refuse a request, and records the refusal with its reason. A refusal is not a judgement on the requester's conduct; it is a decision about the mark.

**5.4 No standing.** Prior conduct, including distribution of the Work, never implies a grant, and no request is answered by default.

## 6. How an objection is handled

**6.1 Reporting.** An objection is raised through the project's published reporting address, and identifies the specific use complained of, the clause of this policy it breaches, the materials, and the address of the party. A report is one link to make.

**6.2 Notice and cure.** The first response is a friendly written notice identifying the specific use and the clause breached, with a **fourteen-day cure period**, recorded. The ordinary case is a well-meaning fork that has not rebranded, and the answer to that case is a note, not a proceeding.

**6.3 Escalation, cheapest first.** The enforcement chain is layered so the project's cost stays proportionate to the threat.

| Layer | What it can do | What it cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| **Norm-setting** | Publish this policy, a canonical NOTICE wording and a canonical rebrand checklist, so compliance is trivial and obvious | Act against anyone who has not read it |
| **Notice and cure** | A template letter and an issue-tracker template, a published reporting address, a recorded cure period | Reach a party that ignores the notice |
| **Platform referral** | A package index, container registry, code-hosting provider or domain registrar may take the listing down or transfer it | Decide the underlying rights question |
| **Formal proceedings** | Contractual and statutory remedies in the appropriate forum, costed, used only when the cheaper layers have failed | Substitute for the cheaper layers; this is a last resort, not a first move |

**6.4 The instrument used.** Apache-2.0 Section 6 withholds every trademark right from every recipient of the Work; the policy states the permitted uses, the rebranding requirements, the cure procedure and the grant process; common law and unfair-competition law support a confusion claim within the market the project operates in; registration and trademark law, when and if any filing is made, supply statutory presumptions and remedies. **No layer of this chain requires registration to be effective now.**

**6.5 No registration assumed.** Nothing in this policy depends on a registration having been sought or obtained. The triggers for revisiting that position are recorded in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).

## 7. The project will not sell Veritas

**7.1 The commitment.** Veritas is free, for life, and will not be sold — not by the project, not by anyone else, in any edition, at any price, under any support tier.

**7.2 What that means in practice.** There is no enterprise edition, no licence key, no feature gate, no paid tier, no per-seat or per-node metering, and no paid add-on for a Capability the product itself provides. The Plugin ecosystem is commercial in the ordinary way — an author may charge for their Plugin — but the marketplace takes nothing, and no Plugin may be sold as Veritas or as a paid add-on for a core Capability.

**7.3 The relationship between §7 and the rest of this policy.** The commitment in §7 is a commitment of the project, not a prohibition on third parties. A third party may fork Veritas, may charge for the fork, and may do so under any name it chooses. **What no one may do is sell something called Veritas.** That is the whole extent of the reservation, and it is what this policy exists to hold.

**7.4 A fork that wishes to keep the mark.** A fork that wishes to keep the Veritas name may apply under §5 for a written, revocable, time-limited permission. The project is under no obligation to grant it, and a fork that rebrands needs no permission at all.

## 8. Status of this policy

**8.1 Adopted.** This policy is adopted with ADR-045 and is in force from its first commit, with no registration required for it to have effect.

**8.2 Where the rationale lives.** [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) is the decision record: it contains the rationale, the enforcement-chain analysis, the deferred-registration position and the triggers for revisiting it. **This document is the policy itself.** Where a question is one of rationale or evidence, `GOVERNANCE.md` governs; where a question is one of obligation, this document governs.

**8.3 The release artefact.** This file is the one that ships. It travels with the release artefact alongside `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`, and it must be kept in step with the decision record it implements.

**8.4 Changing this policy.** A change is a superseding decision, proposed in the same way as any other decision, and recorded in the decision register. This policy is not amended in place.
